# Avyukt Raghuvanshi

**Digital hardware · Semiconductor design · Engineering education**  
Honors Electrical Engineering at the University of Minnesota, College of Science and Engineering.

I build digital hardware and the tools and learning material that help people understand it. My work spans processor architecture, matrix accelerators, FPGA implementation, open-source ASIC flows, and scientific computing. I lead [Stone Arch Silicon](https://github.com/Stone-Arch-Silicon) at UMN and contribute to the [ASIC Network](https://github.com/ASIC-Network).

[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/raghuavy) · [Portfolio source and interactive demos](https://github.com/raghuavy/about_me)

## Building a community around chip design

**[Stone Arch Silicon](https://github.com/Stone-Arch-Silicon)** — our UMN semiconductor-design community. The [club site](https://github.com/Stone-Arch-Silicon/stone-arch-silicon), [ASIC 101 course](https://github.com/Stone-Arch-Silicon/ASIC_101), [documentation book](https://github.com/Stone-Arch-Silicon/ASIC-docs), and [shared documentation theme](https://github.com/Stone-Arch-Silicon/ASIC-Docs-Theme) connect projects with a path for new contributors.

**[ASIC Network](https://github.com/ASIC-Network)** — collaborative infrastructure for people learning and building chips: a [community website](https://github.com/ASIC-Network/asic-network), [analog tutorials](https://github.com/ASIC-Network/analog-tutorials), [digital tutorials](https://github.com/ASIC-Network/digital-tutorials), and [benchmarked-silicon planning](https://github.com/ASIC-Network/benchmarked-silicon).

These are team projects. Repository histories credit the people who build them.
